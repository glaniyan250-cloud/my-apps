Purpose

LOG Holdings Inc. is a diversified holding company. It buys existing businesses across industries, from any seller, and holds them for the long term. Acquired companies keep running independently, with their existing management, while LOG trains the next generation of managers.

Startups do not belong in Holdings. New ventures belong to LOG Ventures Inc.

How this manual works

Chapters 1 to 3 decide whether a deal happens and on what terms. Chapter 4 decides where the cash goes. Chapters 5 and 6 govern the company after purchase. Chapters 7 and 8 set who decides and what Group sees each month. Appendix A works the numbers. Appendix B is the whole manual on one page.

The order never changes: debt is paid first, always. No distribution, bonus, or new acquisition comes ahead of a debt payment.

Chapter 1

1  Buy Filters

A business qualifies for acquisition only if it clears all four filters. A business that fails a filter is not bought, unless Group approves a written exception under Chapter 7.

FilterStandard
Cash marginAt least 12% cash margin, measured on the cash definition below, after the general manager (GM) replacement cost.
Proven recordAt least 3 years of proven cash flow. The rule is flat for all deals — there is no longer tier for large deals.
Regular yearsThe 3 years must be regular years. An extraordinary year — a windfall contract, a one-off boom, a freak year — does not count as proof and is stripped out of the test and the price.
Customer concentrationNo single customer above 5% of revenue.

The cash definition

Every rule in this manual uses the same definition of cash. Cash is not EBITDA.

Cash = revenue collected, minus:

  • all operating costs, including a market GM salary where the business is owner-run;
  • tax;
  • maintenance capital expenditure only — what is needed to keep the business as it is, not growth spending.

The result is free cash before acquisition debt. Acquisition debt is paid from it. What remains is the distributable pot divided under Chapter 4.

Growth spending is funded separately: from the company’s retained earnings under Chapter 4, or by Group approval.

The GM test

For an owner-run business, restate the numbers with a market salary for a replacement GM before applying the 12% test and the 5x price cap. A business that only clears 12% because the owner works for less than market pay does not clear 12%.

TermStandard

Worked shape of a max-price deal

On revenue of 100, a business at the 12% floor produces cash of 12. The maximum price is 60. The buyer pays 15 down and finances 45. Appendix A works this deal in full, including what the financing costs each year and what is left to distribute.

Discipline

  • Price follows proven, regular cash — never a seller’s best year.
  • If the price only works above 5x, the deal does not proceed at that price.
  • The down payment and the financing mix are set deal by deal, inside the 25 / 75 structure above.

Chapter 3

3  Debt Laws

Three laws govern all debt in LOG Holdings. They are absolute. Chapter 7 allows exceptions to the buy filters; no exception can touch these laws.

Law 1 — Acquisitions only. Debt is taken on to acquire businesses, never to fund operations. Operating companies self-finance.
Law 2 — Gone within 7 years. Every acquisition debt is paid off within 7 years of the purchase.
Law 3 — Debt before everything. Debt is paid before any distribution. “We always pay our debt.”

What the laws mean in practice

  • A company in difficulty does not borrow to cover operating costs. Group intervenes under Chapter 6 instead.
  • No layer of the group — company, Holdings, or Business Group — receives a distribution in a period where a due debt payment has not been made.
  • Deal terms are tested against Law 2 before signing: if the financed balance cannot be cleared within 7 years from the company’s proven cash, the price or the structure is wrong.

Why the capital sits at Business Group

Acquisition decisions are made at Group level, not at Holdings level. Cash follows decision rights. Holdings is a lean management layer paid on its 5% fee; it is not the capital holder. Holding the acquisition fund centrally keeps control and sound judgement in one place and keeps cash from lying idle in a country Holdings.

Timing

Companies report monthly. Cash sweeps and distributes quarterly.

Chapter 5

5  Post-Acquisition Improvement

Target: +5 percentage points of margin on every acquisition — for example, 12% to 17%. Not 5% relative.

After every acquisition, Group and management actively look for two kinds of lever:

  • Eliminate inefficiencies — costs, waste, and process that add nothing for the customer.
  • Increase prices — where the offer supports it, price to the value delivered.

The clock

MilestoneStandard
PlanImprovement levers are identified during diligence and the first 100 days, and sequenced into a plan.
Full activation100% fully activated by month 30.
Why 30 monthsSome levers are constrained by contractual obligations — leases, supplier terms, customer pricing agreements, labour contracts — that must run off or be renegotiated before the lever can move.

Live example

North Sea Fish Market

LOG Holdings’ first operating company is already running this rule: margin is being raised from 40% to 45% by increasing prices. That is the same +5-point move this chapter requires of every future acquisition.

Why it matters

The debt payment does not change when margin rises, so the improvement falls almost entirely into the distributable pot. Appendix A shows the effect on a max-price deal: the pot rises from 3.65 to 8.65 on revenue of 100, an increase of 137%.

Progress against the plan is reported monthly — see item 6 of the reporting standard in Chapter 8.

Chapter 6

6  Underperformer Ladder

The ladder applies when a company’s cash margin falls below 12%. Debt is still paid first at every step.

Why the triggers are early: at a max-price purchase, annual debt service takes about 70% of cash at a 12% margin. At 9% it takes 92.8%. At 8% it takes 104.4% — more than all of the cash. There is no room to wait for a full bad year. See Appendix A.

StepTriggerAction
1 — WatchBelow 12% for 1 quarterBonuses pause. Distributions pause. Management explains the cause in writing: temporary, management, or structural.
2 — InterveneBelow 12% for 2 consecutive quartersGroup turnaround plan. Growth spending frozen. Holdings may replace the GM with a LOG-trained manager.
3 — DecideNo return to 12% and no clear path back after the 2-year recovery windowGroup decides: fix, restructure, or sell while the business still has value.
Emergency override: if cash goes negative, or a debt payment is at risk, Group intervenes immediately. The ladder timeline does not apply.

Judgement rules

  • One extraordinary bad year alone is not a sell trigger, just as one extraordinary good year was not proof at purchase. Cause decides.
  • Fix what is fixable. Sell what is structural.
  • While a company is below 12%, its paused bonuses and distributions stay paused until it returns to 12%.

Chapter 7

7  Governance and Exceptions

Where decisions sit

DecisionOwner
Acquisition decisionsLOG Business Group level — not Holdings level.
Deal approvalDammy personally approves every acquisition, for now. Approval thresholds for others will be added as the Group team grows.
Company managementEach country Holdings manages its operating companies for its 5% fee under Chapter 4.
Growth spending above retained earningsGroup approval.

Structure

LOG Holdings Inc. sits under LOG Business Group Inc. Holdings is the only subsidiary registered country by country — Canada first, then the United States, Nigeria, the United Kingdom, Australia, Brazil, and others. Each country’s Holdings directly owns the operating businesses in that country. The other subsidiaries — LOG Capital, LOG Ventures, LOG Properties, and LOG Investments — form a local entity only where a specific business case requires it.

Exceptions

Buy filters — exceptionable, case by case. Group may approve a written exception to the 12% cash test, the 5x price cap, the 3-year rule, or the 5% customer cap. Holdings cannot grant its own exception.

Debt laws — no exceptions. Acquisitions-only debt, cleared within 7 years, paid before any distribution. No approval, at any level, can override these.

An exception is recorded with the deal: which filter, by how much, and why. An exception changes that deal only. It does not change the standard.

8  Monthly Reporting Standard

One page per company, every month. Six items. The same format for every company in every country.

#ItemWhat it shows
1Revenue and cashRevenue and cash per the Chapter 1 definition, with the cash margin %.
2DebtDebt balance, and confirmation the payment was made.
3Largest customerLargest customer as a % of revenue.
4Cash in bankCash balance at month end.
5Red flagsAny red flag: GM issue, lost customer, major breakdown, or equivalent.
6Improvement progressMargin now against the +5-point plan, and what activates next.

What the report feeds

  • The underperformer ladder (Chapter 6) — items 1, 2, and 5 trigger the steps.
  • The quarterly sweep (Chapter 4) — items 1, 2, and 4 set the distributable pot.
  • Exception and approval decisions (Chapter 7) — the same six items are the record Group decides from.

If an item has nothing to report, it says so. A blank item is a missing report.
