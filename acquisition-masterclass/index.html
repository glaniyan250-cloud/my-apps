Buy Filters

A business qualifies only if it clears all four filters. Fail one, and the deal stops — unless Group approves a written exception (Module 7).

Cash is not EBITDA. Cash = revenue collected, minus all operating costs (including a market GM salary where the business is owner-run), minus tax, minus maintenance capex only — what keeps the business as it is, not growth. The result is free cash before acquisition debt.

The GM test: if the owner works for less than market pay, restate the numbers with a market GM salary before testing. A business that only clears 12% because the owner underpays himself does not clear 12%.

Regular years only. A windfall contract or one-off boom is stripped out of the test and the price. The 3-year rule is flat — no longer tier for big deals.

Price & Deal Structure

Maximum price: 5× annual cash flow — cash as defined in Module 1, after the GM adjustment.

Price follows proven, regular cash — never a seller's best year. If the price only works above 5×, the deal does not proceed at that price.

The standard structure is 25% cash down, with the remaining 75% split between seller financing and bank debt. That split is flexible, deal by deal — there is no fixed target mix.

3

Debt Laws

Three laws govern all debt in LOG Holdings. They are absolute — Module 7 allows exceptions to the buy filters, but no exception can touch these laws.

Law 1 — Acquisitions only. Debt is taken on to acquire businesses, never to fund operations. Operating companies self-finance.
Law 2 — Gone within 7 years. Every acquisition debt is paid off within 7 years of purchase.
Law 3 — Debt before everything. Debt is paid before any distribution. “We always pay our debt.”

In practice: a company in difficulty does not borrow to cover operating costs — Group intervenes under Module 6 instead. And deal terms are tested against Law 2 before signing: if the financed balance cannot clear within 7 years from proven cash, the price or the structure is wrong.

The Cash Waterfall

The split applies only to cash remaining after debt payments. Debt service comes first, in full, every period. What is left is the distributable pot.

Why the capital sits at Business Group: acquisition decisions are made at Group level, so cash follows decision rights. Holdings is a lean management layer paid on its 5% fee — not the capital holder. Central capital keeps control and sound judgement in one place, and cash never lies idle in a country Holdings.

By purpose, the split is: 55% compounding (acquisitions + LOG Investments), 20% retained, 5% staff, 10% foundation work, 10% management and admin across all layers. Companies report monthly; cash sweeps quarterly.

Post-Acquisition Improvement

Target: +5 percentage points of margin on every acquisition — 12% to 17%. Not 5% relative.

After every acquisition, Group and management hunt two kinds of lever: eliminate inefficiencies — cost, waste, and process that add nothing for the customer — and increase prices where the offer supports it.

The levers are identified during diligence and the first 100 days, sequenced into a plan, and must be 100% fully activated by month 30. Thirty months, not sooner, because some levers are locked by contracts — leases, supplier terms, pricing agreements, labour contracts — that must run off or be renegotiated first.

The live proof: North Sea Fish Market, the group's first operating company, is already running this rule — margin being raised from 40% to 45% through price increases.

Why it matters: the debt payment does not change when margin rises, so the improvement falls almost entirely into the distributable pot.

6

Underperformer Ladder

The ladder applies when a company's cash margin falls below 12%. Debt is still paid first at every step.

Why the triggers are early: on a max-price deal, debt service takes 69.6% of cash at a 12% margin. At 9% it takes 92.8%. At 8% it takes 104.4% — more than all the cash. There is no room to wait for a full bad year.

Step 1 — Watch (1 quarter below 12%): bonuses and distributions pause. Management explains the cause in writing — temporary, management, or structural.

Step 2 — Intervene (2 consecutive quarters below): Group turnaround plan, growth spending frozen, Holdings may replace the GM with a LOG-trained manager.

Step 3 — Decide (no return to 12% and no clear path back after the 2-year recovery window): Group decides — fix, restructure, or sell while the business still has value.

Emergency override: cash negative, or a debt payment at risk — Group intervenes immediately. The timeline does not apply.

One extraordinary bad year alone is not a sell trigger, just as one extraordinary good year was not proof at purchase. Cause decides: fix what is fixable, sell what is structural.

7

Governance & Exceptions

Acquisition decisions are made at LOG Business Group level — not Holdings level. Holdings manages; Group decides.

Dammy personally approves every acquisition, for now. Approval thresholds for others will be added as the Group team grows. Growth spending beyond a company's retained earnings also needs Group approval.

Exceptions — flexible on buying: Group may approve a written exception to a buy filter — the 12% cash test, the 5× price cap, the 3-year rule, or the 5% customer cap — case by case. Holdings cannot grant its own exception. An exception is recorded with the deal: which filter, by how much, and why. It changes that deal only, never the standard.

Absolute on debt: no exception, at any level, can touch the three debt laws — acquisitions only, cleared within 7 years, paid before any distribution.

Structure: LOG Holdings Inc. sits under LOG Business Group Inc. Holdings is the only subsidiary registered country by country — Canada first, then the US, Nigeria, the UK, Australia, Brazil, and others — and each country's Holdings directly owns the operating businesses there.

Reporting Standard

One page per company, every month. Six items. The same format for every company in every country.

1. Revenue and cash (per the Module 1 definition), with the cash margin %  ·  2. Debt balance, and confirmation the payment was made  ·  3. Largest customer as % of revenue  ·  4. Cash in bank  ·  5. Red flags — GM issue, lost customer, major breakdown  ·  6. Improvement progress — margin now against the +5-point plan, and what activates next.

The report feeds everything: items 1, 2 and 5 trigger the underperformer ladder; items 1, 2 and 4 set the pot for the quarterly sweep; the same six items are the record Group decides exceptions from.

If an item has nothing to report, it says so. A blank item is a missing report.
